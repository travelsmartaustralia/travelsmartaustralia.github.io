Tokyo and Japan first-timer travel guide

Food markets, neighbourhoods and day trips. Practical ideas for planning your first visit.

Travel Smart Australia • Independent travel guide
Travel requirements, prices, weather and opening times change. Confirm important details with official sources before booking.

What to see and do

Explore Asakusa and Shibuya, plan a day for museums or gardens, and allow time for neighbourhood food spots.

Getting around

Rail connections are extensive; check station exits and luggage rules before busy transfers.

Where to stay

Choose accommodation based on the places you want to visit, transport access, recent independent reviews and the full cost. Check cancellation terms and any additional charges.

Suggested three-day outline

  • Day 1: Arrive, settle into your accommodation and explore the nearby neighbourhood.
  • Day 2: Visit the main attractions that interest you, grouping nearby stops together.
  • Day 3: Explore a different area, enjoy local food and leave time for an unhurried departure.

Before you travel

  • Confirm passport, visa and entry requirements using official government sources.
  • Compare flights, accommodation locations and transport options.
  • Consider suitable travel insurance and check exclusions.
  • Check seasonal weather and local customs.

Japan first-timer itinerary

Japan in seven days: Tokyo, Kyoto and Osaka

Seven days is enough for highlights but not every attraction. Stay near a convenient rail station and avoid packing too much into transfer days.

DaySuggested route
1Arrive in Tokyo; explore your hotel neighbourhood
2Tokyo: Asakusa, Senso-ji and nearby streets
3Tokyo: Shibuya, gardens or museums
4Train to Kyoto; explore central Kyoto
5Kyoto: Fushimi Inari and historic districts
6Osaka: Dotonbori, food streets and city sights
7Depart from Osaka, or allow extra time to return to Tokyo

Suggested accommodation areas

In Tokyo, compare hotels near Ueno, Shinjuku or Tokyo Station based on your arrival airport and itinerary. In Kyoto, staying near Kyoto Station or central transit connections can simplify travel. In Osaka, Namba and Umeda are useful bases for different priorities.

Budget considerations

Compare airfare options before choosing an open-jaw itinerary. Budget for intercity rail, local transport, accommodation, food, attraction tickets and travel insurance. A national rail pass is not automatically the cheapest option; compare the cost against individual tickets for your route.

Photo stops and practical tips

Early mornings can be useful for photographing popular Kyoto streets; always respect local photography restrictions. Consider Asakusa at dawn, Tokyo skyline viewpoints, Fushimi Inari paths and Osaka's illuminated Dotonbori area.
